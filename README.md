
# Digital Transformation and Decarbonization

**Author:** Sahar Motallebi  
**Institution:** Linnaeus University, Sweden  
**Course:** 4IK524  
**Project type:** Individual academic literature review  
**Field:** Information Systems, Digital Transformation and Sustainability

## Overview

This literature review examines the role of digitalization in decarbonization, focusing on the technological, organizational and societal dimensions of sustainability transitions. It investigates how digital technologies can support emissions reduction and resource efficiency while also creating environmental, institutional and social challenges.

## Research Questions

1. How does digitalization contribute to decarbonization and resource efficiency across different sectors?
2. What challenges and trade-offs accompany the implementation of digital technologies for sustainability?
3. What frameworks or practices can maximize the environmental, social and economic benefits of digital solutions?
4. How can policymakers and organizations address the resource-intensive nature of digital technologies while pursuing decarbonization?

## Methodology

The project uses a structured literature review to examine empirical research on digitalization and decarbonization.

The analysis considers technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), the Internet of Things (IoT), blockchain and data analytics. Findings are synthesized across technological, organizational and societal dimensions to identify recurring patterns, implementation challenges and research gaps.

The review also develops a conceptual framework connecting digital technologies with organizational processes and sustainability outcomes.

## Main Findings

The literature identifies several potential contributions of digitalization:

- Improved resource monitoring and operational efficiency.
- Data-supported environmental planning and decision-making.
- Opportunities for emissions reduction through digital infrastructure and services.

However, the review also identifies limitations and trade-offs:

- Energy consumption and environmental impacts of digital infrastructure.
- Organizational resistance and implementation challenges.
- Digital inequality and unequal access to technological benefits.
- Privacy, governance and ethical concerns associated with digital systems.

The findings emphasize that the environmental benefits of digitalization depend on how technologies are designed, governed and implemented.
